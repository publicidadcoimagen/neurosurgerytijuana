Spine Surgery in Tijuana

Spine surgery may be considered in patients with persistent pain, nerve compression or neurological symptoms related to conditions affecting the spine. Not every patient requires surgery, but a proper evaluation can help determine when a surgical option may offer a meaningful and safe benefit.

Many patients search for spine surgery in Tijuana when they have chronic back pain, pain radiating into the legs, weakness, numbness or difficulty with daily activities. A specialist consultation helps identify the cause of symptoms and determine whether treatment should remain conservative or whether surgery may be appropriate.

When might spine surgery be considered?

Spine surgery may be evaluated when symptoms do not improve with conservative treatment, when there is significant nerve or spinal cord compression, or when pain has a major impact on quality of life. In some cases, progressive weakness, walking difficulty or other neurological changes also make specialist assessment especially important.

Common conditions that may lead to spine surgery evaluation include herniated discs, spinal stenosis, certain cases of spinal instability, nerve compression and advanced degenerative spine conditions.

Symptoms that may require neurosurgical evaluation

  • Persistent lower back or neck pain
  • Pain radiating into the arms or legs
  • Numbness or tingling
  • Muscle weakness
  • Difficulty walking or standing for long periods
  • Reduced ability to perform daily activities

These symptoms do not automatically mean that surgery is necessary, but they do indicate the value of a complete evaluation by a spine or neurosurgical specialist.

Diagnosis before spine surgery

Before considering any procedure, it is important to perform a detailed medical history, neurological examination and imaging studies such as MRI, CT scan or X-rays, depending on the case. Proper diagnosis helps determine which structure is responsible for symptoms and what treatment strategy may be most appropriate.

An important part of the consultation is distinguishing between patients who may improve with conservative care and those in whom surgery could offer better symptom relief or functional recovery.

Conservative treatment and surgical options

In many patients, initial treatment includes medication, physical therapy, activity modification and clinical follow-up. When these measures do not adequately control symptoms, or when neurological symptoms worsen, surgical options may be discussed.

The type of surgery depends on the specific diagnosis. In some cases, the goal is to relieve pressure on a nerve; in others, to stabilize the spine or address a more complex structural problem. Decisions should always be based on the clinical picture and imaging findings, not on pain alone.
